WHAT THE TRACE SHOWS

—

Round-robin sends each family to both workers. Cache-aware routing keeps each family together in this run. Once both workers are warm, round-robin is fast too.

The trade-off is still ahead.

Cache-aware placement can concentrate work. Sequential traffic does not expose competing queues. Concurrency and low-reuse controls are the next experiments.

METHOD & LIMITS

A small experiment you can inspect.

What stays the same

Exact request bodies, model weights, worker image, CPU limits, and streaming client. Each policy starts with restarted workers and a fresh gateway. No warmup; retries disabled.

What we measure

Monotonic client time to first nonempty content chunk, final cached-token usage, and SMG’s routed-worker header. Server prompt time is reported separately from TTFT.

What we cannot claim

One seed, 12 requests per policy, no concurrent traffic, no cache-capacity pressure. Synthetic labels distinguish otherwise shared warehouse text. No GPU or production throughput claims.

Why the median looks dramatic

Six round-robin requests are slow and six are fast. Its median lies between those groups. A median ratio does not mean every request sped up by that amount. Inspect the individual bars and mean alongside cache accounting.
